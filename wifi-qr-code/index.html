How to make a Wi-Fi QR code

  1. Enter your network.

    Copy the Wi-Fi name and password exactly, including capital letters and spaces. Choose WPA / WPA2 for a personal network, WEP for a legacy network, or No password for an open network. Mark Hidden network only if your router hides its name.

  2. Save your code.

    The QR appears as you type. Download a PNG, or select Print / Save PDF and use your browser’s print dialog. Keep the classic black code for easy scanning, or customize its frame.

  3. Scan. Confirm. Connect.

    On a compatible phone, open the camera or QR scanner, point it at the code and confirm the join prompt. Test the saved image with your actual network before printing it for guests.

PLAIN ANSWERS.

Wi-Fi QR code questions

Do you store my Wi-Fi password?

No. The network name and password are processed in this browser tab and never uploaded to JustMakeQR. Basic visit statistics do not include these details. We do not save your network details to browser storage. Your browser may retain form state according to its own settings.

Can someone read the password from the QR code?

Yes. The Wi-Fi QR contains the network name and, for a protected network, its password. It is an easy way to share access, not encryption. Anyone with the image can decode it. Share a guest network rather than a private network where appropriate.

Will it work on iPhone and Android?

Many current iPhone and Android camera or QR-scanner apps recognize Wi-Fi codes and offer a join prompt. Support depends on the phone, scanner and network settings. If you only see text, try the phone’s built-in QR scanner. Always test with the phones your guests use.

What security option should I choose?

Use WPA / WPA2 (personal) for networks with a shared password, WEP for a legacy WEP network, or No password for an open network. This tool does not configure enterprise networks with individual logins or certificates. WPA3-only networks may need another sharing method; compatibility depends on your device and router.

Does the code expire?

JustMakeQR sets no expiry date and requires no subscription. The code works only while the encoded Wi-Fi name, password and network settings remain valid. Change those details and you need a new QR image.

Can I create a QR for a hidden network?

Yes. Check Hidden network to include the hidden-network flag. Your device must support that flag and be within range of the network. If joining fails, confirm your router settings and test another compatible scanner.

Why does scanning fail to connect?

Check the exact network name, password, security type and hidden-network setting. Spaces count. Make sure the network is in range and that the phone supports it. For printed codes, keep a clear white margin and high contrast. Some networks also require a separate web login after connecting.

Can I print a Wi-Fi sign or download a PDF?

Yes. Download PNG saves the QR image. Print / Save PDF opens your browser’s print dialog, where you can select Save as PDF. Add a frame or caption under Customize your QR, then scan-test the finished design at its printed size.

What stays private

Your QR content is not our data. We don’t upload or store the text, URLs, contact details, Wi-Fi passwords or logos you enter. The generator runs in your browser, with temporary working state in this tab. There is no account database or saved-code dashboard.

Basic visit statistics. UnjamKit uses Google Analytics to understand page visits, traffic sources and device/browser types. Google Analytics uses first-party cookies, configured here to expire after 90 days. Enhanced measurement and advertising personalization are disabled. We do not send your files, filenames, pasted text, lists, QR content or Wi-Fi passwords. Page URLs sent by our tag exclude query strings and fragments; referrers are reduced to their origin. See Google’s privacy policy.

The hosting boundary. The hosting provider serves this website and may retain standard request logs, including IP addresses, as part of operating the service. That is separate from your QR content, which the tool does not transmit. Your browser may also retain history, downloads or form state according to its own settings.

The code is meant to be read. Anyone who can scan your QR code can read what is encoded in it. A linked website or service has its own privacy practices. Don’t put secrets in a QR code you plan to share publicly.
